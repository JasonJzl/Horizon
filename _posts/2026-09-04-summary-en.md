Developer rabahs describes using Claude Fable 5 to port his 1993 MC68000 Amiga game, originally written in Baghdad, to the Godot engine over a summer holiday. Claude assembled its generated code with vasm on the author&\#x27;s Mac and iterated until the output was byte-identical to the original, apart from a 108-byte difference. Rabahs attributes that mismatch to his original AsmOne workflow: the game ran in memory and saved that memory to disk, so the shipped files were snapshots of an already-running game, not clean assembler output. He then spent weeks feeding Claude his notes, git repos, and 33-year-old memories to produce an article draft, which he edited line-by-line over a week; the port&\#x27;s final feel and shipping took additional weekends and evenings. The original Babylonian Twins game is being released for free.

hackernews · rabahs · Sep 3, 14:28 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49550375)

**「Background」** Babylonian Twins is an Amiga game from 1993 written in MC68000 assembly, a low-level language often used on 16-bit home computers for performance and tight memory control. Retrogames of that era frequently lack preserved source code, so porting them normally requires manual reverse engineering or disassembly. This case is notable because an LLM reconstructed the assembly into byte-identical output before the code was translated to a modern engine, providing verifiable confidence in the conversion.

**「Impact」** For retro developers and reverse engineers, the case demonstrates that LLM-assisted assembly-to-Godot porting can be completed quickly on small original binaries and can be made verifiable by comparing generated binaries directly with original artifacts, though the author notes he independently verified only the binary matching step and not every later analytical claim.

### [Classifying Price Manipulation in Nonlinear Transient Impact Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02447) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 3, 04:00

**「Background」** The paper studies transient impact models built by composing an instantaneous nonlinear law with a memory kernel. Whether price manipulation can be ruled out depends on the order of this composition, and earlier rigidity theorems only worked for kernels bounded at zero, leaving the singular case open.

**「Solution」** Lee classifies both possible orders. When a nonlinearity acts on the trading rate before a nonzero integrable Volterra kernel, imposing nonnegative cost on every finite piecewise-constant round trip forces the law to be affine, and linear for every nonzero convolution kernel. The argument uses a zero-volume chattering pump read out by two thin baseline trades, which extends the rigidity to singular kernels. For the power law sgn\(x\)\|x\|^δ with power-law decay t^\{-γ\}, 0&lt;γ&lt;1, manipulation exists exactly when δ≠1; in particular, square-root impact is manipulable at all decay exponents, and Gatheral&\#x27;s two-block bound δ+γ≥1 collapses to the line δ=1. When instead a monotone readout acts on the impact state after the kernel, safety for all inputs and readouts is equivalent to complete positivity of the kernel, with a constructive converse, so square-root impact after power-law memory is manipulation-free. A remote compensating block shows that round-trip safety and all-input safety coincide for kernels with uniformly vanishing tails and differ for permanent memory, and the mechanisms classify two-mode Prony kernels, first-order time-inhomogeneous memory, and matrix memory while quantifying friction and switching complexity.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that absent friction, concave impact laws such as square-root impact are safe only when they enter after the memory kernel, not before it. Calibrated exponent pairs all lie in the manipulable set, making the paper&\#x27;s rigidity and complete-positivity criteria a sharp guide for designing non-manipulable transient impact models.

### [Switching Frictions and Heterogeneous Horizons in Persistent Order Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02525) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 3, 04:00

**「Background」** Persistent, long-memory order flow is an empirical regularity that is not fully explained by models in which traders decide instantaneously. The author argues that costly changes in the representations investors use for portfolio choice, combined with heterogeneous switching thresholds, can act as an additional source of persistence without appealing to slow-moving information.

**「Solution」** The mechanism treats each trader&\#x27;s portfolio-choice representation as a state that is changed only when the cost of switching is outweighed by opportunity-driven incentives; because thresholds differ across traders, residence times in one representation vary and form a renewal process. Aggregating order flow over these spells maps the execution-weighted tail of the spell-duration distribution into the decay of aggregate flow covariance. Under common execution weights, first-passage renewal analysis shows one tail determines three linked quantities: the duration-spell memory exponent, the order-flow memory exponent, and the horizon at which finite-market scaling must stop. Structural simulations recover these restrictions from generated paths, quantify how using mismatched weights distorts the estimated memory, and show that finite cross sections shorten the effective inference horizon. The resulting protocol turns a fitted aggregate persistence parameter into cross-dataset restrictions that can test whether a duration-based kernel is appropriate for a separate execution-cost model.

**「Takeaway」** The broader contribution is to make long memory in signed order flow a structural consequence of switching frictions and heterogeneous horizons, with joint restrictions that can be checked against realized trading data rather than merely fit to aggregate autocorrelations.

### [LLM Agents in Double Auctions: Slower Convergence and Public Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02580) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 3, 04:00

**「Background」** The authors ask whether large language models, increasingly deployed as economic agents, can participate in market mechanisms designed for humans, and whether those mechanisms still deliver intended outcomes when humans are replaced by LLMs. They address this by replicating seminal economic experiments in a double auction, a widely used market mechanism.

**「Solution」** The authors test whether markets populated by LLM agents converge to market equilibrium and allocate resources efficiently. They report that such markets exhibit slower or no convergence toward equilibrium and provide less efficient allocations than markets populated by humans. Analyzing individual trading decisions reveals substantial heterogeneity across both model families and market roles. A lexical analysis of Chain-of-Thought traces suggests that deciding to execute a trade rather than continue incrementally adjusting prices is associated with a shift from strategic considerations toward urgency. The authors also publicly release their testing framework for future evaluations of LLM agents in market settings.

**「Takeaway」** The authors&\#x27; core message is that compatibility with core market mechanisms is a largely unexamined dimension of LLM alignment, and current evidence indicates LLM agents fall short of human trading efficiency. Their released framework makes this market-alignment assessment repeatable for future model evaluations.

### [Machine-Learned Measures Distinguish Liquidity-Supplying and Liquidity-Demanding HFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00858) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin.TR \(Trading &amp; Microstructure\) · Sep 3, 04:00

**「Background」** Public data do not identify high-frequency trading directly, and standard proxies fail to separate liquidity-supplying from liquidity-demanding strategies. This measurement gap makes it difficult to study how different sides of HFT affect market quality using widely available data.

**「Solution」** The authors address this by training machine learning models on proprietary Nasdaq data, learning a mapping from observed HFT activity to public intraday variables. Applying this mapping produces daily measures of liquidity-supplying and liquidity-demanding HFT for all U.S. stocks from 2010 to 2023. These measures largely subsume standard proxies and capture time-series variation those proxies miss. Using proprietary Euronext Paris data, the authors show the approach generalizes across markets and remains predictive years after training. With the resulting 14-year panel, they find supply-side HFT is consistently associated with greater pre-announcement information acquisition, more informed trading, and lower bid-ask spreads, while demand-side HFT is associated with the opposite patterns. During COVID-19, HFT-supplied liquidity remained resilient, and its association with lower spreads strengthened.

**「Takeaway」** The authors conclude that machine learning trained on proprietary data can overcome the lack of HFT identifiers in public datasets, enabling cross-market, long-horizon measurement. Their findings support the view that liquidity-supplying HFT tends to improve market quality, while liquidity-demanding HFT does not.

### [Can Public Data Recover LMF Order-Splitting?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30999) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 3, 04:00

**「Background」** Market order flow exhibits long-range correlations, commonly explained by the Lillo-Mike-Farmer \(LMF\) order-splitting theory: institutions break large trades into smaller pieces. However, prior quantitative tests needed proprietary trader identifiers, so reproducibility and cross-market validation were limited.

**「Solution」** The authors ask whether LMF-consistent order splitting can be recovered from anonymous public data using synthetic metaorder reconstruction. Using transaction and quote data for the 239 largest JSE stocks by market capitalisation as of 13 March 2026, covering 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2025, they run a grid search over reconstruction parameters. Each configuration is scored against established metaorder stylised facts and the LMF relation. Configurations chosen to minimise errors on aggregate impact stylised facts reproduced these aggregate properties but gave a poor LMF relation. Configurations chosen to minimise the LMF discrepancy recovered that relation and retained several broad impact features, but had weaker stock-level execution and decay fits. The authors argue the asymmetry shows aggregate impact stylised facts alone cannot identify LMF-consistent order splitting, and that their LMF-targeted result is best interpreted as establishing compatibility within the reconstruction class, not an independent validation.

**「Takeaway」** The authors treat their findings as consistency with, rather than proof of, LMF theory when using anonymous public data. In their view, the theory remains plausible, but reconstructing metaorders from public data does not uniquely identify LMF-style order splitting.

### [Fake Stationary Regimes for Stochastic Volterra Equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03474) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 3, 04:00

**「Background」** Stochastic Volterra integral equations \(SVIEs\) with affine drift appear naturally in rough volatility modeling, but their memory-dependent kernel makes classical stationarity and long-run confluence theory difficult to apply. The authors address whether such processes can still exhibit useful stationarity-like behavior, both over finite horizons and asymptotically.

**「Solution」** The paper introduces a notion of a “fake stationary regime” in which all marginal distributions share the same expectation and variance. The authors show this can be engineered in two ways: through explicit closed-form choices of the deterministic initial condition and mean-reversion function, or by adding a deterministic stabilizing factor to the diffusion coefficient while leaving the mean-reversion function flexible. They then study L^p-confluence, asking whether marginals from different initial values become asymptotically close as time grows, and establish functional weak long-run asymptotics: time-shifted solutions converge weakly to a family of L^2-stationary processes with the same covariance function. These results are applied to exponential-fractional SVIEs with an α-gamma kernel for α ∈ \(0,1\], the rough-path regime, and are used to propose a family of stabilized rough volatility models.

**「Takeaway」** The authors argue that even without a fully Markovian stationary state, SVIEs with affine drift can support controlled stationarity-like regimes via marginal moment matching and functional weak limits. This provides a theoretical basis for designing stabilized rough volatility models that remain analytically tractable.
