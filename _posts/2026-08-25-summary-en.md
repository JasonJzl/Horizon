### [Canonical Ethereum Traces Enable Protocol-Agnostic Arbitrage Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20377) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 25, 04:00

**「Background」** Ethereum arbitrage detection usually relies on protocol-specific patterns, which makes tools brittle and hard to generalize across protocols and chains. The authors argue that transaction traces themselves admit a canonical structural form, so fund-flow equivalence can be decided without hardcoding protocol logic.

**「Solution」** Khayam and colleagues build each execution trace into an abstract syntax tree of token transfers grouped by call-frame nesting, then reduce it with a convergent term rewriting system of 15 rules to a unique canonical form. They mechanize in Rocq that the system is terminating, sound, confluent, and that the induced structural equivalence on fund flows is decidable, with zero admitted obligations. Because cycles emerge at fixpoint, arbitrage detection needs no protocol-specific events and runs unmodified on Arbitrum and BSC using only standard ERC and WETH ABIs. In an evaluation over 220,000 Ethereum blocks against Eigenphi and 1,000 shared blocks against ArbiNet, the system produced 469,801 confirmed detections and 245,497 attempted arbitrages, agreeing with Eigenphi on 83.5% of detections and covering 81% of ArbiNet, while surfacing 60,199 exclusive confirmed detections. 99.2% of detections come from the fixpoint alone and are sound by construction; manual validation of 500 transactions found no false positives in the confirmed tier. Reanalysis of 200 Eigenphi-exclusive detections showed 63.5% have no cycle in canonical form, and 9.0% have cycles the fixpoint detects but the conservative classifier does not.

**「Takeaway」** The authors show that protocol-agnostic, formally verified structural equivalence on fund flows is practical and already competitive with production systems, opening the door to strategy-family classification, bot fingerprinting, and equivalence-based attribution rather than pattern matching.

### [Crypto Mean Reversion at 15 Minutes: Strong but Untradeable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin.TR \(Trading &amp; Microstructure\) · Aug 25, 04:00

**「Background」** Kitron and Wengrowicz set out to test whether short-horizon mean reversion, a weak but documented pattern in US equities, is actually present in cryptocurrency markets under a matched, strictly out-of-sample protocol.

**「Solution」** Using 15-minute returns, they find the effect is far stronger and more pervasive in crypto: 90% of 183 Binance pairs show significant directional reversal versus 2.7% of 187 US stocks and ETFs, across every focal coin-year since 2021. The signal lives in signs, not magnitudes: lag-one return autocorrelation is near zero on major coins, yet betting against the previous candle captures most of the reversal. US-listed funds whose net asset value is a crypto or metal price inherit the underlying&\#x27;s reversal, while merely correlated stocks inherit nothing, suggesting a wrapper&\#x27;s tape reads like the process it wraps, not the venue it prints on. On the originating tape, the reversal concentrates after moves driven by aggressive taker flow and grows with flow intensity, consistent with compensated liquidity provision. The gross edge peaks near 1.3 basis points per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost, so the pattern is large enough to detect but too small to clear realistic benchmark spot capture costs. The contrast survives an artifact battery, an exact permutation null, and a frozen six-month holdout.

**「Takeaway」** The authors conclude that short-horizon crypto mean reversion is statistically overwhelming yet economically unexploitable, and that its conditioning on taker flow and wrapper ETF behavior offers transferable market-microstructure insight beyond this specific market.

### [Loop-Gain Matrix versus Scalar Stability Monitoring for Leveraged ETF Complexes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22768) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin.TR \(Trading &amp; Microstructure\) · Aug 25, 04:00

**「Background」** Monitoring leveraged ETF stability usually tracks a scalar loop gain for each fund&\#x27;s daily rebalancing, treating cross-asset spillovers as bias. The author argues this is structurally insufficient when funds trade correlated underlyings as a coupled feedback system.

**「Solution」** Modeling the complex with a matrix gain L, the author shows scalar monitoring misses two effects: cycle amplification, because the spectral radius rho\(L\) is at least each diagonal gain and strictly larger under two-way coupling, and transmitted displacement, which appears even with one-way coupling and is invisible to the receiver&\#x27;s own gain. He proposes a reduced-form estimator of L from prices and public fund assets — no order flow — via cross-asset overnight reversals, and documents measurement-convention sensitivity. Simulations recover the spectral radius \(RMSE 0.005 at T=250\), a lead-lag confounder causes only 2% false alarms, and in a blind-spot configuration scalar says &\#x27;safe&\#x27; while matrix says &\#x27;unsafe&\#x27; on all paths. In the 2026 Korean single-stock LETF episode, the estimator detects transmission from SK Hynix into Samsung&\#x27;s closing price \(DiD z=-2.82, randomization p=0.0055 vs 182 controls\), scaling with sender capital, with about 41% of Samsung&\#x27;s closing displacement variance imported despite Samsung&\#x27;s own &\#x27;moderate&\#x27; gain of 0.24. The same procedure returns nulls for the deeper US MSTR-Bitcoin-Coinbase venue.

**「Takeaway」** Stability monitoring should be organized around the complex-by-venue matrix rather than individual products. Scalar per-product gains understate coupled feedback by construction, so system-level spectral-radius monitoring is needed.

### [Marketron Model Identifiability and Generalized Langevin Equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20589) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 25, 04:00

**「Background」** Itkin addresses the Marketron model&\#x27;s structural non-identifiability, where an eighteen-parameter space traps solvers in suboptimal local minima and renders economic quantities unmeasurable. The prior option-pricing extension of the model suffers from this same problem, motivating a principled reduction.

**「Solution」** Itkin removes exact scaling gauges and sign symmetries, freezes non-financial parameters through explicit criteria, and adiabatically eliminates the fast hidden signal, producing a robust nine-parameter reduced model. A Gauss-Newton Hessian with empty null space and a manifold-boundary analysis confirm that the reduced core carries no exact symmetry and admits no further reduction. A diffusive correlation between flow and return innovations captures the short-maturity skew, and a staged calibration from the physical to the risk-neutral measure, illustrated on SPX options, fits the whole surface with a single parameter set. The wedge between physical and pricing values of the flow block becomes a well-defined market price of flow risk, identifiable for the first time though only weakly constrained by a single surface. The reduction also shows that log-price obeys a generalized Langevin equation with a closed-form, state-modulated memory kernel, whose memory variable is the exact Markovian lift; this yields a testable condition—the equality of signal and memory relaxation rates—which on the SPX surface come out well separated but weakly identified, placing the fitted market tentatively in the driven, non-equilibrium regime.

### [Public Wallet Identities Reveal Informed Trading on DEXs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04373) ⭐️ 8.0/10

**「Background」** In traditional market microstructure, informed traders are supposed to need anonymity so they can hide among the uninformed. The author exploits a decentralized exchange that publishes the persistent pseudonymous wallet address behind every committed order, cancellation, rejection, and fill, making the usual anonymity assumption testable.

**「Solution」** Reconstructing the full-depth limit order book from 17.1 billion messages, 14.3 million aggressive orders by 147,113 wallets, and $84.3 billion in taker notional, the author ranks wallets by the price movement following their aggressive orders. This informativeness is persistent: the ordering of wallets remains stable across adjacent ten-day windows, with a rank correlation of 0.52. Adding the live activity of the highest-ranked wallets to a standard anonymous benchmark of prices, quotes, and order flow raises the out-of-sample R2 for one-second returns to 12.31%, a 13.2% gain \(t = 9.2\) that is 1.6 times larger than the best of 200 activity-matched placebo cohorts. The improvement grows when measured at realized trades rather than at every sampled moment, from 1.43 to 2.47 percentage points of R2. These results indicate that public wallet histories contain short-horizon price information that anonymous order-book data leave unmeasured.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that public wallet identities carry economically significant information beyond anonymous order-flow data, implying that pseudonymity does not fully protect informed traders from revealing their intentions. This makes decentralized exchange data a powerful setting for studying adverse selection and price discovery.
