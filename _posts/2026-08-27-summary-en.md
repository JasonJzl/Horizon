### [PDE Hedging in General 1D Diffusion Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25223) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 27, 04:00

**「Background」** In mathematical finance, hedging in diffusion markets typically starts from a classical stochastic differential equation \(SDE\) for the underlying asset. The authors instead consider general one-dimensional diffusion markets characterized solely by their scale function and speed measure, which may not admit a classical SDE representation, while keeping the interest rate constant.

**「Solution」** The authors develop a PDE-based methodology that derives a hedging equation whose solution generates a self-financing hedging strategy. They provide sufficient conditions on the scale, speed, and interest rate under which this strategy attains the minimal hedging capital, expressed via the no free lunch with vanishing risk \(NFLVR\) condition. They also prove necessary and sufficient conditions for NFLVR and characterize the class of equivalent local martingale measures through an auxiliary diffusion determined by the real-world diffusion and the interest rate. When NFLVR fails, the framework can produce multiple hedging equations corresponding to non-minimal strategies, whose associated prices may exceed the minimal capital. Numerical experiments with diffusion models having irregular features illustrate both the effectiveness and the limitations of the approach.

**「Takeaway」** The authors&\#x27; central thesis is that scale-and-speed data alone can drive a rigorous PDE-based hedging framework, yielding explicit hedging strategies and NFLVR characterizations even without an SDE representation. This broadens the toolbox for pricing in irregular diffusion markets, while cautioning that when NFLVR fails, pricing and hedging become ambiguous and potentially suboptimal.

### [Task-Specific AI Boundaries in PhD Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25678) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 27, 04:00

**「Background」** Artificial intelligence is increasingly embedded in scientific work, but it has not been clear whether researchers judge its use uniformly across all research tasks. Angelini and Lyrvall argue that a simple acceptance-versus-rejection framing is insufficient for understanding how researchers actually view AI.

**「Solution」** The authors analyze an international, self-selected sample of 3,785 PhD students in STEM and medical and health sciences from Nature&\#x27;s Graduate Survey 2025, measuring comfort with AI for writing research articles, collecting and analyzing data, designing experiments, tracking literature, and summarizing it. Using latent class analysis, they identify four distinct attitudinal profiles. The dominant profile reflects a &quot;division of labour,&quot; where AI is widely accepted for literature-related tasks but resisted for activities tied to intellectual contribution, such as writing, data analysis, and experimental design. The other profiles are &quot;status quo&quot; \(broadly uncomfortable\), &quot;all-purpose&quot; \(broadly comfortable\), and &quot;undecided&quot; \(substantial uncertainty\). The authors interpret these patterns as task-specific normative boundaries around delegation, authorship, and responsibility, while cautioning that because the survey measures comfort rather than legitimacy, the profiles should be treated as attitudinal configurations with a normative dimension.

**「Takeaway」** The core conclusion is that attitudes toward AI in research are organized less around a simple acceptance-rejection divide than around task-specific boundaries. This suggests that AI governance, doctoral training, disclosure, and research evaluation should adopt task-specific approaches rather than blanket policies.

### [Output-Only Identification and Spectral Monitoring of Coupled Feedback Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25844) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 27, 04:00

**「Background」** Coupled feedback networks are often monitored channel by channel, even though cross-channel paths alter stability margins and transmitted disturbances. The author studies identification of a structured feedback matrix L\_t = Phi diag\(gamma\_t\) in an output-only setting, where no commanded, probing, or reference input exists and only temporally separated outputs and scheduling gains gamma\_t are observed.

**「Solution」** The identification rests jointly on the persistent excitation of the observed pre-window output and on two structural features: the known time variation of gains, which modulates the closed-loop response in a predictable pattern, and a partial-reversal moment by which a known fraction of transient displacement is corrected in a subsequent window. The author provides a hierarchy of results: exact local identification under a Jacobian rank condition on the gain regimes; a first-order interaction estimator whose identification strength is the minimum eigenvalue of the residualized interaction information matrix and is provably unidentified under constant gains; and a characterization of the estimand as a resolvent sensitivity, which is suited for screening transmitted disturbances and serves as a first-stage input to spectral-margin recovery. The estimator has sqrt\(T\) asymptotics, a cross-identification theorem maps each varying gain to exactly identified resolvent rows and columns, and bootstrap validity holds under consistent selection; the implemented heuristic&\#x27;s empirical coverage of 90% at nominal 95% quantifies the remaining gap. Simulations verify sharpness of the rank condition and quantify benchmark failures under confounding, and a case study on leveraged-fund rebalancing feedback uses daily fund disclosures as known gains.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that output-only identification and spectral monitoring of coupled feedback networks are achievable using temporally separated outputs and known time-varying gains, allowing cross-channel path monitoring without probing inputs. The small but real gap between heuristic and nominal coverage highlights a practical limitation in the proposed estimator.

### [A self-hosted quant scoring engine overturns retail trading instincts](https://www.reddit.com/r/algotrading/comments/1vz08cn/i_spent_a_year_building_a_selfhosted_quant/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

reddit · r/algotrading · /u/Unlucky-Minimum-2480 · Aug 26, 15:26

**「Background」** The author spent a year building a self-hosted quant scoring engine because they wanted financial scoring, FCF inflection data, and macro supply-chain narratives as clean, independent inputs—without SaaS lock-in or assembling half a dozen data sources. The results come from their own backtest harness on cached daily candles; some tiers use a simulated AI-accuracy ceiling, so the author flags those sim numbers as directional rather than exact.

**「Solution」** Across measurements, the findings ran against retail instincts. A Friday report grading weekend gap-down risk as trim, no action, or monitor stops beat both blanket flatting and blind holding. In high-volatility regimes, widening the gap exit from 3% to 6% beat panic-exiting the open. A/B testing showed close-through stops beat wick stops: return 113 vs 78, drawdown 17.4% vs 19.6%, and stop-outs down 38%, while gap-through exits stayed because an open beyond the stop means real slippage. Overtrading was the largest alpha drain: 82% of exits were forced rotations, including rotating out of NVDA at +314% and netting roughly zero; slowing the machine to a 3–20 day minimum hold, requiring 2.5x score gain, and capping the signal set won across bear, recovery, and AI-bull windows \(64.8% vs 16.3% vs 35.1% in one sample\). Sizing conviction was also backwards: losers entered 28–30% larger, and measured news conviction predicted almost nothing; bullish 6.5+ scores were anti-predictive at +5 days \(41.6% hit\), while bearish 4.5-and-below scores were the strongest avoid/exit signal at +10 days \(71% hit\).

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s meta-lesson is that rules of thumb are compressed conclusions from someone else&\#x27;s data. Rather than following them, a system should measure its own data and let the results override even confidently held retail instincts.

### [Wealth, Social Networks, and Inequality Across 46 Cultures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25488) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 27, 04:00

**「Background」** The author notes that while theory links wealth inequality to social structure, empirical evidence has been limited to a few studies using online social media data. This leaves open the question of whether such patterns hold across diverse, face-to-face societies.

**「Solution」** To address this, the study collected data from roughly 3,500 sharing units \(households\) in 46 communities across the globe, spanning considerable social and cultural diversity. In each community, the author examined how material wealth relates to social network connections formed through borrowing money, sharing food, working together, socializing, and similar interactions. In nearly all communities, a sharing unit&\#x27;s wealth was positively associated with the number of other units it both helped and was helped by. Wealth was also associated with the relative wealth of connected units, a form of economic homophily. Notably, communities with greater wealth inequality showed network structures in which poorer units were less well connected to wealthier ones. The dataset was augmented with environmental, institutional, and economic attributes at the community level, opening new avenues for studying how wealth and social networks co-evolve.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that wealth and social network structure are closely intertwined across cultures, with inequality reflected in the segregation of poorer and wealthier households. This cross-cultural evidence suggests that economic homophily is a widespread feature of human social life rather than an artifact of online interactions.

### [Hawkes-Driven OTC Market Making via Volterra-Riccati Approximation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02002) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Aug 27, 04:00

**「Background」** Over-the-counter market makers face request-for-quote \(RFQ\) flows that are clustered and long-memory, making the control problem path-dependent. Exact Markovian lifting is impractical for general or long-memory Hawkes kernels, so the author needs a tractable approximation for optimal quote setting.

**「Solution」** The author develops a hierarchy of Volterra-Riccati approximations to handle general Hawkes RFQ arrivals and controlled fill thinning. The first level replaces future request flow with its conditional Volterra forecast, the second adds a covariance correction for intensity uncertainty, and the third updates the quote rule using the realized Hawkes memory. In an exponential-kernel benchmark where the exact lifted HJB can be solved numerically, the state-feedback Volterra-Riccati policy closely tracks the exact solution, particularly in directional regimes, while a memory-free Poisson policy suffers substantial regret. Applied to a power-law-like RFQ memory model, the policy converts directional RFQ bursts into persistent quote skew via the continuation-value shadow price, producing endogenous quote impact with long-memory decay and improved inventory and P&amp;L risk control relative to a no-conditioning Poisson benchmark.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that Volterra-Riccati state-feedback policies offer a practical, accurate approximation for path-dependent OTC market making under clustered and long-memory RFQ dynamics, capturing persistence that memory-free benchmarks miss.
