### [Adaptive Singular-Point Method for Pricing Surrenderable Equity-Linked Contracts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01323) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 2, 04:00

**「Background」** Pricing and hedging surrenderable equity-linked life-insurance contracts is hard because the accumulated fund does not recombine, while periodic premiums, guarantees, and early surrender interact with stochastic volatility and interest rates. Conventional approaches that build a full multidimensional fund lattice become computationally prohibitive under these features.

**「Solution」** The authors avoid a full fund lattice by discretizing volatility and interest-rate factors on recombining lattices while representing the contract value at each factor node as an adaptive one-dimensional function of the fund. Periodic contributions become translations of the fund argument, and surrender is enforced through a backward obstacle condition. Piecewise-cubic representations are used to propagate payoff and exercise singularities, with continuous pruning criteria controlling approximation error. The method is shown to converge weakly, and on regular fund regions it achieves Delta consistency; for the strict binomial scheme, first-order weak accuracy and a Talay-Tubaro expansion support Richardson extrapolation. Numerical experiments with independent Monte Carlo and least-squares Monte Carlo benchmarks demonstrate compact representations and favorable cost-accuracy tradeoffs. Hedging results also reveal that contracts with similar values can expose the insurer to materially different equity, volatility, and interest-rate risks.

**「Takeaway」** The authors argue that valuing realistic surrenderable equity-linked contracts does not require a full multidimensional fund lattice; an adaptive one-dimensional representation coupled with recombining factor lattices is both accurate and computationally viable. Their work also emphasizes that similar contract values do not imply similar risk exposures, making accurate hedging at least as important as accurate pricing.

### [Evaluating Autonomous Factor-Discovery Systems in Asset Pricing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00731) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 2, 04:00

**「Background」** Recent advances in LLM agents enable a new paradigm called Agentic Empirical Asset Pricing \(AEAP\), where systems autonomously conduct scientific discovery. However, existing evaluation practices backtest only the outputs—factors or trades—rather than the autonomous discovery system that produced them, leaving reliability of the system itself unexamined.

**「Solution」** The authors focus on factor discovery and contribute a reference architecture, a rigorous evaluation standard for discovered factors, and a method for out-of-sample backtesting the discovery system. As a concrete instance, they evaluate SEADS against five re-implemented baselines on two US equity panels. Their central methodological finding is that no single metric ranks the systems consistently, so evaluation must proceed on multiple axes simultaneously. They also run a separate rolling re-execution to ask whether the discovery process itself—not just one static output—is reliable. The paper reports negative findings and limitations that expose further evaluation pitfalls for future AEAP systems, underscoring that claims require stress-testing beyond a single output snapshot.

**「Takeaway」** The authors argue that asset-pricing research should evaluate the autonomous discovery system rather than only its outputs. Their work makes the case that rigorous, multi-axis, out-of-sample evaluation is necessary before trusting such systems.

### [A Year of Rigorous BTC Signal Testing Ends Mostly Null, One Simple Strategy Survives](https://www.reddit.com/r/algotrading/comments/1w4wfrv/search_for_btc_daily_trading_signals_is_coming_to/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

reddit · r/algotrading · /u/Affectionate\_Tea3401 · Sep 2, 01:36

**「Background」** The author spent about a year reconstructing Bitcoin&\#x27;s UTXO-level history from raw chain data and deriving Glassnode-like on-chain metrics, then ingested 296 source metrics and roughly 3,000 engineered features to hunt for tradeable daily signals. After building a strict statistical falsification pipeline, almost every candidate failed.

**「Solution」** Every experiment followed a pre-registration lock: finalize a protocol, timestamp it, run synthetic-only tests, then abandon failed locks forever; voided tests stayed in a ledger and counted against multiplicity, while walk-forward and purged k-fold splits, BH-FDR control, permutation falsification, and realistic costs were applied throughout. The funnel shrank from 364,014 univariate tests and 91,500 Granger/CCF tests to 5 post-FDR candidates, zero after falsification; 49 ML scorecards all came back NO-GO, and intraday 1h/4h/8h strategies failed under adversarial perturbation. Only two strategies survived: buy-and-hold, a 50-day MA, and “Markov + MA-50,” which is not ML but a 4D discrete state space \(MVRV-Z, NUPL, trend, volatility regime\) whose exposure is sized by that state&\#x27;s expanding-window 90-day mean forward return, gated by the MA-50. On 2021–2026 out-of-sample data with 20 bps round-trip costs, Markov + MA-50 improved Sharpe to 1.08, Calmar to 1.25, and cut max drawdown from −76.6% \(buy-and-hold\) to −31.2%, winning 25 of 27 sensitivity configurations and surviving permutation falsification on 7 of 8 horizons. Elsewhere, macro liquidity was real but context-only, and the popular “0.75 liquidity overlay” was spurious because all drift-matched random walks reached \|correlation\| ≥ 0.75.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s larger point is pessimistic and methodological: after this scale of transparent testing, no daily on-chain signal survived rigorous falsification, and he suspects many published on-chain “signals” are in-sample fits. The surviving edge came from simple regime conditioning on cycle state, not from another predictive feature.

### [Latent-Space No-Arbitrage Geometry for Implied Volatility Generative Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00332) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 2, 04:00

**「Background」** Generative models are increasingly used to produce implied volatility surfaces, but their outputs must respect static no-arbitrage conditions that rule out economically impossible option prices. The authors move this constraint from the output domain into the generative model&\#x27;s latent space, setting up the boundary problem they then analyze.

**「Solution」** The authors assign every latent code a scalar margin determined by whether the generated surface obeys no-arbitrage conditions; codes with nonnegative margin constitute the admissible latent set. They establish that, under stated conditions, strictly admissible codes remain admissible under small perturbations, and the admissible set&\#x27;s boundary is precisely the zero-margin set. For regular boundary components they formulate a level-set equation whose local dynamics push toward that zero-margin boundary, which lets practitioners locate and correct problematic codes. Because the analysis treats the generator only as a deterministic map from latent variables to surfaces, it is architecture agnostic and applies to variational autoencoders, generative adversarial networks, and similar models. Numerical tests recover known boundaries in analytic examples. In experiments with a variational autoencoder trained on Heston surfaces, the authors find that comparable reconstruction errors can correspond to different admissible regions, and that the latent prior can concentrate inside the admissible region. They also show that the computed boundary can be used to modify latent codes that currently generate violating surfaces.

**「Takeaway」** The authors&\#x27; central claim is that no-arbitrage validity is a latent-space geometric property, not merely an output constraint: zero-margin latent codes lie on a learnable boundary whose dynamics can repair violating samples. This offers a unifying, architecture-independent route for enforcing financial constraints in generative volatility models.

### [Unpriced Operational Risk Falls on DeFi Depositors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00911) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 2, 04:00

**「Background」** DeFi protocols, like banks, expose depositors to operational risk — the author cites USD 9.45 billion in losses across 1,075 events since 2020 — but unlike banks, they are not required to hold capital. Some protocols maintain voluntary buffers; absent one, the residual risk falls on depositors, who should demand compensation in the supply yield. Whether the market actually prices that tail is the open question this article tests.

**「Solution」** The author fits a per-sector Basel-style loss-distribution model to a new dataset of operational-risk events and tests both margins—voluntary buffers and yield spreads—against it. Core sector tails fall inside the Moscadelli banking band \[0.85, 1.39\], while Bridge, Derivatives, and residual Other sectors show cyber-loss-level tails with estimated tail indices near 1.6, in some cases beyond the infinite-mean boundary. For lending, the implied VaR\_\{99.9\} capital buffer is 18% of total value locked; among the ten largest lending venues, only four hold a buffer, covering on average about 5% of that need. Empirically, venues without a buffer do pay a higher median yield than buffered ones—a 125-basis-point gap—so market discipline works in the right direction, but the premium still falls far short of fair tail compensation. The author concludes this unpriced tail falls disproportionately on retail depositors, who see posted rates but cannot price the risk, and recommends standardized disclosure of losses, existing buffers, and tail coverage rather than bank-style capital mandates.

### [A Fast-Excursion Heston Limit and Its Impact on Barrier Option Hitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06737) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 2, 04:00

**「Background」** In the classical Heston stochastic-volatility model, very fast volatility reversion is usually handled through conventional weak limits under Skorokhod topologies, which describe price paths as continuous or càdlàg. The author argues this view is insufficient when the quantity of interest is not a vanilla option value but the chance that a continuously monitored barrier is hit, because rapid excursions matter exactly where standard weak convergence smooths them away.

**「Solution」** McCrickerd introduces a fast-excursion Heston model, obtained from Heston under Mechkov&\#x27;s fast-reversion limit, that is a non-degenerate limit escaping the Skorokhod topologies. At every instant the process makes an excursion through an interval of prices, leaving it interval-valued rather than a single random path. Using selections in the theory of random closed sets, he constructs these interval-valued processes as lifts of subordinated Lévy processes. The model can be simulated with price-time parametric representations, and convergence is visualized through a purpose-built classical Heston simulation scheme. The abstract reports that taking excursion risk into account raises barrier hitting probabilities considerably, with an impact on the order of 10% for one-month EURUSD barrier options, although no supporting data appears in the excerpt.

**「Takeaway」** For the author, excursion risk that is invisible to vanilla prices can materially change hitting probabilities for continuously monitored barrier options, motivating a shift from ordinary price paths to interval-valued representations. The model also stands as a rare theoretical example of a non-degenerate stochastic-volatility limit that escapes the usual Skorokhod framework.
