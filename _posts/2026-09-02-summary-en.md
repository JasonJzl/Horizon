### [Quality-Adjusted Price Index for AI Inference Services](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29843) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 1, 04:00

**「Background」** Official-style price measurement for AI inference typically applies matched-model methods that track prices of identical products over time. As models are renamed, upgraded, and replaced quickly, those methods cannot capture the quality change that makes a falling nominal price misleading, so the true pace of AI price declines has been unclear.

**「Solution」** The author builds a quality-adjusted price index from 21,024 posted-price observations across 3,208 models and 86 providers, linked to 4,605 benchmark scores through a latent quality index estimated from benchmark response patterns; this replaces the hedonic tradition&\#x27;s product characteristics with evaluations of model capability. Measured by matched-model methods, inference prices fell at 0.10 log points per year, while the quality-adjusted index fell at 0.73, meaning 87% of the decline is invisible to current statistical practice. On a per-task basis, however, the buyer&\#x27;s price has stopped falling: reasoning models consume tokens faster than token prices decline, so seller-side and buyer-side prices diverge. A pre-registered validity audit disciplines the quality measure, and its sharpest result is that excluding contamination-flagged benchmarks leaves model rankings essentially unchanged \(correlation 0.998\) yet moves the index by 0.49 log points per year, undercutting leaderboard-stability arguments for using benchmarks in economic statistics. Prices, quality, and the audit are fully reproducible from public sources at zero cost.

**「Takeaway」** The broader point is that conventional price statistics dramatically understate the real decline in AI inference quality-adjusted prices, while buyer-side task costs have plateaued; benchmark-based quality adjustment, although reproducible and powerful, needs its own economic validity audit before it can support competition and productivity measurement.

### [A Real-Data Reality Check for Deep Hedging on Bitcoin Options](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29025) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 1, 04:00

**「Background」** Classical option hedgers like Black-Scholes delta assume costless, continuous rebalancing, while deep hedging trains networks to handle frictions directly; prior claims of superiority were mostly tested against frictionless baselines on simulated prices.

**「Solution」** Using five years of Deribit BTC options data \(2020-2024\), the author compares Black-Scholes delta, Leland, and Whalley-Wilmott against LSTM and feedforward deep hedgers with CVaR loss and an optional trading penalty, all facing a 5 basis point transaction cost. Across 11,546 test episodes from September 2023 to December 2024, Whalley-Wilmott saved $1.79 per episode versus plain BS delta \(95% CI \[-2.21, -1.39\], p &lt; 0.0001\) while trading about eight times less often; its P&amp;L and tail-risk improvements were directionally better but not significant at that sample size. None of the deep models beat any classical benchmark on any metric, and they kept trading almost every hour even when penalty weights spanned a twenty-fold range. In a calmer validation period, Whalley-Wilmott&\#x27;s P&amp;L edge shrank or disappeared while its cost advantage held, indicating regime dependence. The author attributes the failure to a small training set and the lack of any built-in mechanism for sitting still in the architectures tested.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that deep hedging&\#x27;s simulated advantages do not survive a fair empirical comparison under realistic market frictions. Honest evaluation on real data across market regimes is essential before adopting these models.

### [Portfolio Risk Bounds from Language-Model Representations without Return Covariances](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29692) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 1, 04:00

**「Background」** Portfolio risk assessment usually depends on reliable estimates of cross-asset return covariances, but short, high-dimensional panels make those estimates difficult to obtain. The authors propose instead using distribution-valued firm characteristics derived from language-model representations to certify portfolio risk.

**「Solution」** The authors derive a sharp upper bound on systematic portfolio variance, and a corresponding bound for standardized returns, using multi-firm Wasserstein-2 optimal-transport dispersion. This requires maintaining links from characteristics to systematic exposures and from exposures to returns. To make the approach practical, they introduce a weighted pairwise relaxation that yields an objective that is convex under a checkable condition and needs only marginal volatility scales, not cross-asset return covariances. With zero firm-specific slack, the common-map scale changes the certified variance reduction but not the normalized allocation, which depends only on observed information geometry. In a 52-firm panel from 2018-2022, an allocation built from Qwen3-Embedding-8B news representations landed between the 0.69th and 1.33rd in-sample variance percentiles across four prespecified capped portfolio populations, while equal risk weighting landed between the 21.1st and 28.6th percentiles. The lower in-sample variance ranking relative to equal risk also persisted across the reported frozen language-model representations.

**「Takeaway」** The authors&\#x27; central thesis is that distribution-valued language-model representations can turn firm-level information into a coherent risk bound and an implementable allocation rule without cross-asset return covariances. This provides a practical route for portfolio risk certification in high-dimensional settings where covariance estimation is unreliable.

### [Neural Shrinkage of Indefinite Correlation Matrices for Small-Cap Portfolios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30446) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 1, 04:00

**「Background」** Small-cap-inclusive equity universes include recently listed and intermittently traded securities, so requiring a common look-back period discards a substantial share of available price information. Pairwise-complete estimation preserves the longest overlap for each asset pair, but because entries are computed on different samples, the resulting correlation matrix can be indefinite, which prevents its direct use in Markowitz optimization and falls outside the assumptions of standard random-matrix shrinkage.

**「Solution」** The authors adapt a rotation-invariant neural covariance estimator to this setting. The model first computes mask-aware marginal moments and a pairwise correlation matrix proxy, then processes its signed spectrum and uses a bidirectional gated recurrent unit conditioned on factor-aligned effective sample lengths derived from the overlap matrix and eigenvector loadings. It maps all eigenvalues, including negative ones, to a positive inverse spectrum, yielding a positive-definite reconstructed covariance that is trained end-to-end to minimize five-day realized global-minimum-variance risk. They evaluate 26 expanding-window models from 2000 to 2025 on up to 1,500 U.S. equities in a closing-auction simulator with point-in-time selection, commissions, financing, corporate actions, and market impact. Across the 26-year out-of-sample period, the neural estimator reduces annualized five-day volatility by roughly 20% and increases the Sharpe ratio by roughly 40% relative to the next-best covariance estimator. These improvements hold across realized risk, risk-adjusted performance, and drawdown control, persist after modeled execution frictions, and are supported by a 99.9% Model Confidence Set that retains only the neural estimator.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s central thesis is that a neural estimator trained end-to-end can transform indefinite pairwise-complete correlation inputs into a positive-definite covariance, outperforming conventional alternatives for small-cap-inclusive portfolios. This suggests that handling the signed spectrum directly, rather than discarding pairwise-complete information, can yield material gains in realized risk and Sharpe ratio.

### [Separating AI Inference from Execution Authority in Agentic Finance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30519) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 1, 04:00

**「Background」** As AI agents increasingly choose tools, counterparties, and transaction parameters, a fundamental question arises: should the ability to infer a course of action also grant the authority to execute a financial action? The authors argue that inference should not confer authority, and they identify a need for explicit control mechanisms to keep agentic finance bounded and accountable.

**「Solution」** The authors develop Authority-Inference Separation \(AIS\), an intent-centered architecture that treats a financial action intent as the control object. A machine-generated proposal can receive temporary executable authority only after an independent deterministic control plane validates registered agent identity, accountable ownership, mandate and risk-appetite lineage, policy version, state, approvals, and exact economic semantics. Blockchain then enforces the operational representation of granted authority and records settlement evidence, while institutional legitimacy, service delivery, accounting classification, and human accountability remain off-chain obligations. Evaluation combines four-domain instantiation, official BIS and MAS cases, a 48-fixture executable prototype, and a public-ledger observability test. Across 36 synthetic authorization attacks, a direct-agent baseline accepted 36 attack effects, a prompt-policy baseline accepted 20, and AIS accepted none, while all three accepted 8/8 admissible fixtures. AIS also rejected 4/4 token replays and 8/8 recipient or rail substitutions, withheld completion in 4/4 service-delivery failures, and populated all 13 defined evidence fields. A test of 1,700 recent Base transactions with public x402 facilitator addresses showed that public ledgers can evidence settlement and selected authorization parameters, but cannot establish institutional mandate, legal accountability, service delivery, or accounting treatment.

**「Takeaway」** The authors conclude that AIS and blockchain are complementary: AIS decides whether a specific intent may act, while blockchain can make granted authority bounded, executable, and independently observable. Together they provide a more robust foundation for bounded, accountable agentic finance than relying on inference capabilities or prompt-level policies alone.

### [Battery Storage&\#x27;s Rising Role in GB Balancing Price Formation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29818) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 1, 04:00

**「Background」** Renewables dominate Great Britain&\#x27;s generation mix but rarely set the marginal price, so it is unclear which flexible technologies translate a renewable-rich system into real-time price formation. The authors set out to identify who actually occupies the price-setting edge in the Balancing Mechanism.

**「Solution」** Dalton and O&\#x27;Sullivan reconstruct the price-ranked edge of the eligible bid/offer stack across 50,684 half-hourly settlement periods from 2023 to 2025, attributing marginality to individual Balancing Mechanism Units and separating long-system bid-active from short-system offer-active conditions. They find battery storage rose from 0.8% to 36.2% of bid-active marginality and from 3.2% to 26.9% of offer-active marginality, displacing combined-cycle gas on the bid side and pumped storage on the offer side. Capacity-normalised marginal capture rose on both sides, and unit-quarter fixed effects show an additional 100 MW of Balancing Mechanism active capacity was associated with a 0.55 and 0.48 percentage-point increase in quarterly bid- and offer-side marginal share. By 2025, battery actions were around £9-10/MWh more favourable than volume-matched non-battery alternatives drawn from the same ladder, yet batteries were under-represented by 12.9 percentage points in the highest-priced 5% of short-system periods. Individual batteries remain consistent with price-taking, while the fleet as a whole has become endogenous to routine balancing price formation.

**「Takeaway」** The authors conclude that battery storage is no longer merely a flexibility resource but has become a routine marginal price-setter in the GB Balancing Mechanism, meaning decarbonisation of price formation is already underway through storage participation.
