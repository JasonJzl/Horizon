### [NASA&\#x27;s Roman Space Telescope Set to Launch Aug 30](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/roman-space-telescope/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NASA&\#x27;s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is scheduled to launch on August 30 aboard a Falcon Heavy rocket. Designed for wide-field infrared surveys, Roman&\#x27;s field of view is much larger than Hubble&\#x27;s, enabling it to map vast areas of sky and study dark energy, exoplanets, and galaxy evolution. The mission will generate up to 1.4 TB per day of raw compressed data, and all observations are planned to be fully open to the public with no embargo once processed. This open-data approach is expected to accelerate discoveries in astronomy and support large-scale scientific data processing.

hackernews · JumpCrisscross · Aug 29, 15:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49490870)

**「Background」** The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is a NASA infrared observatory named after former NASA chief astronomer Nancy Grace Roman. Its construction was completed on 25 November 2025, and it is scheduled to launch to a Sun–Earth L2 orbit on 30 August 2026. The telescope has a 2.4-meter primary mirror, the same size as Hubble&\#x27;s, but is optimized for wide-field imaging, enabling large survey programs in dark energy, exoplanets, and infrared astrophysics. All Roman data are planned to be fully open with no embargo, allowing immediate public access to processed observations.

**「Impact」** Astronomers and data scientists will gain immediate access to roughly 30 petabytes of open, wide-field infrared survey data from Roman&\#x27;s five-year primary mission, with no embargo after processing, requiring large-scale data processing pipelines and enabling fast discoveries.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters are enthusiastic about Roman&\#x27;s potential, noting that its wide-field capability and open data will complement observations from Rubin, Hubble, and JWST over the next decade. Some highlight the 1.4 TB/day no-embargo data stream as an opportunity for public discovery, while others credit Roman&\#x27;s reportedly under-budget and ahead-of-schedule development to its heritage as a retrofit of an obsolete spy satellite.

### [DHS Uses Obscure Legal Tool to Obtain Journalists&\#x27; and Nonprofits&\#x27; Records](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/29/trump-dhs-1509-summons-records-journalists-nonprofits) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Department of Homeland Security has been using an obscure legal mechanism, the 1509 summons, to obtain phone and communications records from tech companies without judicial approval, according to a Guardian report. The practice has targeted journalists, non-profits, and unions, raising privacy concerns because there is no judge in the loop. In one case, T-Mobile turned over six months of phone records for journalist Fort, covering more than 10,000 calls and texts, while Google refused to comply. DHS has withdrawn some summonses after court challenges, potentially to avoid a ruling on their legality. This leaves individuals and organizations vulnerable to warrantless surveillance.

hackernews · firefax · Aug 29, 18:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49492219)

**「Background」** Section 1509 of Title 19 of the U.S. Code is an obscure customs-related law that allows the Department of Homeland Security to issue administrative summonses for records without obtaining a judge&\#x27;s approval. Recent reporting indicates the Trump administration has relied on this authority to secretly obtain phone and social media records concerning journalists, unions, and nonprofits. Because DHS has sometimes withdrawn the summons when challenged before a court could rule on its legality, the practice has not yet been definitively tested in court.

**「Impact」** Journalists, nonprofit workers, and union members face the immediate risk that their call and text records will be handed to DHS without their knowledge, as already happened to journalist Fort via T-Mobile.

**「Community Discussion」** Some commenters say DHS withdraws summonses to avoid a court ruling and that companies need not comply, with T-Mobile&\#x27;s compliance contrasted against Google&\#x27;s refusal. One commenter defends the absence of a judge in the loop as an efficiency measure, while another promotes self-hosted infrastructure for journalists.

### [Samsung&\#x27;s Processing-In-Memory Draws Skepticism](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/hot-chips-2026-samsungs-processing) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Samsung&\#x27;s processing-in-memory \(PIM\) technology was presented at a Hot Chips conference, drawing attention while also provoking skepticism about its practicality and novelty. The idea is to place compute directly in memory to reduce data movement, but the approach requires knowing in advance where dependent data resides, which constrains application design. Commenters noted that matrix multiplication still requires extensive data movement, and that many similar accelerator concepts are pitched at trade shows every year without broad adoption. The piece highlights that despite the long history of the concept, Samsung&\#x27;s implementation remains an open question for real-world use.

hackernews · ingve · Aug 29, 06:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49487341)

**「Background」** Processing-in-memory \(PIM\) is a computer architecture approach that places compute units inside or very close to DRAM banks to reduce the energy and latency cost of moving data between separate memory and processor chips. Samsung&\#x27;s LPDDR5X-PIM, presented at Hot Chips 2026, is based on a standard LPDDR5X-9600 DRAM with 16 banks and adds a dedicated PIM block to each bank, enabling in-memory computation for AI inference workloads. This represents a continuation of Samsung&\#x27;s PIM efforts, which have been shown at earlier Hot Chips conferences and are aimed at bringing in-memory processing to a wider range of applications.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters were generally skeptical, saying PIM is not a new idea and only fits a narrow set of workloads such as AI, gaming, and crypto. One commenter recalled seeing a similar Samsung concept at a Hot Chips event in 2020 or 2021, while another acknowledged that processing in memory is &quot;clearly the future&quot; but questioned whether this specific implementation overcomes the data movement bottleneck.
