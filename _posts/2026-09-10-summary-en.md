hackernews · ModelForge · Sep 9, 14:37 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49627370)

**「Background」** Looped transformers, also called recurrent-depth or universal transformers, reuse the same transformer weights across repeated computational steps rather than stacking many distinct layers, which can approximate greater depth while saving GPU memory. In large language models, a “hidden” reasoning trace means the model does not output its intermediate chain of thought, which complicates monitoring. The surrounding discussion concerns OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra, which is described as using a recurrent-depth architecture and as being better than GPT-5.6 Sol at multi-hop reasoning without chain of thought, amid rumors that it hides its reasoning trace.

**「Impact」** For developers and alignment researchers who depend on chain-of-thought monitoring, the key consequence is that looped \(recurrent-depth\) transformers move extra computation into repeated layers at token generation rather than into additional visible tokens, while GPT-6 Astra and comparable LLMs remain RLVR-trained reasoning models that still emit intermediate reasoning traces. Existing hidden-reasoning taxonomies further show that those traces can be shaped to conceal or misrepresent the underlying computation, so monitoring gains partial rather than complete visibility.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters dispute the framing that looped transformers are a special new technique, with one noting they simply reuse weights across stacked layers to save GPU memory and another arguing hidden reasoning is by definition what happens when a model&\#x27;s reasoning trace is looped back into the model rather than emitted. Practical reactions are mixed: one user says Astra&\#x27;s quality changed abruptly after Monday and now feels like Sol, while another calls a real-time MSPAINT computer-use demo jaw-dropping.

### [Nyström Attention Matches Full Attention for Cross-Sectional Stock Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08106) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 9, 04:00

**「Background」** Cross-sectional stock prediction models use inter-stock attention to capture relationships across assets, but this module is parameter-heavy and it is unclear whether its learned structure is genuinely relational or effectively uniform. Guo examines MASTER&\#x27;s inter-stock multi-head attention, which accounts for 42.5% of model parameters and 25% of predictive value.

**「Solution」** Guo decomposes MASTER&\#x27;s inter-stock multi-head attention and finds the learned attention is near-uniform \(perplexity 278/300\), yet forcing exact uniformity eliminates all cross-sectional discrimination. Spectral analysis resolves this paradox: the deviation from uniformity is low-rank \(effective rank ~65, with top-10 modes capturing 96.5% of energy\). This explains why sparse approximations fail while Nyström low-rank attention with m=32 landmarks matches full O\(N²\) attention at O\(mN\) cost, certified equivalent via two one-sided tests at N=300 \(5 seeds, Rank IC p=0.003\) and N=800 \(10 seeds, Rank IC p=0.034\). The author also reports that attention anti-correlates with return similarity \(Spearman rho = -0.614; on the industry-labeled subset, -0.645, and -0.627 after controlling for industry, beta, and volatility\), suggesting complementarity-seeking rather than correlation mining. All graph-based alternatives degrade performance, with hard masking worse than complete module removal. At N ~ 3,500 with adapted architectures, no cross-stock module—GCN, Nyström, or MASTER-style—significantly outperforms a per-stock LSTM baseline \(n=4 seeds\), indicating the benefits observed at smaller scales do not trivially transfer.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that inter-stock attention&\#x27;s value resides in a compressible, dynamic, near-global redistribution that rewards low-rank approximation but resists sparsification, while cautioning that these gains may not persist at larger scales.

### [Entropy Projection Is Numeraire-Invariant; MEMMs Are Not](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08605) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 9, 04:00

**「Background」** Choosing a pricing measure from the martingale-measure set for a given numeraire is not numeraire-neutral: a change of numeraire sends Q to T\_LQ, with density L dQ, where L is the terminal likelihood ratio. The author asks which entropy-based selection rules survive that transform.

**「Solution」** The author shows that the forward relative-entropy projection minimizing D\_KL\(P‖Q\) commutes with numeraire change, since its objective shifts only by the constant −E\_P log L, whereas the minimal-entropy martingale measure \(MEMM\) orientation D\_KL\(Q‖P\) does not; a trinomial counterexample demonstrates that independently recomputed MEMMs need not be likelihood compatible. The two orientations are identified as the Q-dependent terms in the classical convex-dual objectives for logarithmic and exponential utility, and likelihood compatibility is shown equivalent to equality of the pricing functionals obtained in the two numeraires. Consequently the forward selectors price every integrable claim consistently, while the counterexample&\#x27;s two MEMMs assign different prices to a nonreplicable digital claim. A class-level characterization further shows that uniform invariance over elementary one-period likelihood-ratio families forces a smooth convex f-divergence to be logarithmic, up to scaling and affine equivalence. In finite-state markets the forward projection exists under the usual strictly positive feasible-point condition, its density dP/dQ\* is attainable log-optimal terminal wealth, and the minimum forward entropy equals maximal expected log growth.

**「Takeaway」** For the author, numeraire invariance singles out the forward relative-entropy projection rather than the MEMM, linking entropy-based pricing directly to log-optimal terminal wealth and to the duals of logarithmic and exponential utility.

### [A profit-bias identity for sports betting and an MLB test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06739) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 9, 04:00

**「Background」** Sports betting moves money continuously from a large public to a small number of firms, and the influential Levitt \(2004\) account holds that bookmakers price away from the market-clearing point to exploit predictable public biases. That model computes profit from two numbers—the probability a side wins and the fraction of handle it attracts—while assuming the share on a side is independent of the outcome.

**「Solution」** The author relaxes that independence assumption and derives a profit-bias identity: bookmaker profit is affine and increasing in the expected share of handle on the losing side, with Levitt&\#x27;s expression as the independence special case. The identity resolves the book&\#x27;s margin into exactly three channels—hold, price shading times public lean, and covariance between bet share and outcome—implying that a lean is worthless without shading and shading is worthless without a lean. Under a public-belief model, the profit driver is the public&\#x27;s Bayes error, the probability that a representative bettor selects the losing side, and the paper gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a Goldilocks Zone in which book and bettor both profit. Testing these predictions on 1,139 Major League Baseball games, the author finds that the apparent dependence between bet share and outcome is a Simpson&\#x27;s paradox: it is present when games are pooled but absent once games are separated by which side the book favored. The public leans heavily toward favorites, but no matching shading is detected, and the realized margin is indistinguishable from the hold.

**「Takeaway」** By decomposing bookmaker margin into hold, shading-lean, and share-outcome covariance, the paper argues that observed bookmaker profit need not imply exploitable public bias—especially when pooled data produce a Simpson&\#x27;s paradox.

### [Simpler Logistic Model Matches Complex Rivals in LoL Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08060) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · arXiv q-fin \(Quantitative Finance\) · Sep 9, 04:00

**「Background」** Forecasting individual map outcomes in professional League of Legends is a pre-game problem in which the natural instinct is to reach for richer machinery: team strength drifts over time, and side selection and draft choices interact with it. The authors ask whether that complexity is actually necessary for accurate win probabilities.

**「Solution」** Their proposed model is a one-stage logistic regression fit end-to-end on the win/loss log-loss, combining each team&\#x27;s exponentially weighted moving average of past same-side results, a ridge-shrunk stable strength derived as the maximum-a-posteriori estimate of a logistic mixed model, and a first-pick draft covariate; it is natively calibrated out of sample \(walk-forward slope 0.995\) and augments a purely dynamic Bradley–Terry specification with stable team strengths. To test whether that design is sufficient, the authors independently build the strongest rival they can: a two-stage composite mixed model under REML and BLUP shrinkage with Platt calibration. On 5,135 games spanning six regional leagues and three international events \(2024–2026\), paired per-game Diebold–Mariano inference shows the two architectures are statistically indistinguishable on every protocol and window \(global holdout 0.2230 vs. 0.2257; walk-forward 0.2207 vs. 0.2215\), so the authors prefer the simpler model on parsimony rather than accuracy. Both clearly improve on the classical dynamic benchmark \(0.2351\) and more modestly on the static fits \(0.2301/0.2268\). Against Polymarket on 928 matched maps, the forecasts are statistically indistinguishable from the market on its own per-game contracts, with a modest market edge that the authors locate on cross-region Worlds and series-decider maps.

**「Takeaway」** The authors argue that added modeling complexity buys no measurable pre-game accuracy in this setting: a one-stage logistic paired-comparison model matches a carefully constructed mixed-model pipeline and rivals a prediction market, leaving parsimony as the deciding criterion.

**「Background」** Since its inception, Bitcoin has been presented as a revolutionary alternative to national currencies. The paper tests that claim against two competing traditions—Post-Keynesian monetary theory, which anchors sovereign currency in debt-based IOUs and state-enforced fiscal acceptance, and the Austrian School, which evaluates money through Mises&\#x27;s Regression Theorem and the need for an independent, non-monetary commodity anchor.

**「Solution」** Rather than treating either verdict as settled, the author weighs the strongest counterarguments within each tradition. From a Post-Keynesian perspective, Bitcoin lacks the debt-based IOU architecture and state-enforced fiscal acceptance that anchor sovereign currency, though the author notes this framework faces definitional circularity when applied to non-debt commodity monies such as gold. From an Austrian viewpoint, Bitcoin&\#x27;s consistency with Mises&\#x27;s Regression Theorem remains deeply contested: early-adopter subjective utility can theoretically resolve the regression problem, but a strict reading holds that Bitcoin lacks an independent, non-monetary commodity anchor. It reports that Lightning routing reliability has expanded through Pickhardt-Richter multi-path flow allocations, but that this performance gain is structurally concentrated in well-capitalized custodial liquidity hubs, confirming the game-theoretic centralization predicted by Avarikioti et al. \(2020\). The author concludes that Bitcoin&\#x27;s architectural trade-offs, rather than superficial operational metrics, systematically impede its capacity to function as a sovereign monetary standard.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core thesis is that Bitcoin&\#x27;s architectural trade-offs—not its operational metrics—systematically limit its capacity to serve as a sovereign monetary standard. The synthesis leaves Bitcoin&\#x27;s monetary status contested across both theoretical traditions, with Lightning&\#x27;s centralizing liquidity dynamics giving the critique empirical force.
